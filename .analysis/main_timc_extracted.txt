Abstract
Cooperative payload transportation with multiple unmanned aerial
vehicles (UAVs) is challenging because the payload dynamics are
strongly coupled with cable constraints, nonlinear rotational motion,
allocation errors, and external disturbances. This paper proposes
an RBF-ESO backstepping control framework for robust payload-
level trajectory tracking. A nominal backstepping controller is first
constructed to generate the desired payload force and moment
commands. A radial basis function (RBF) neural network is then
introduced to approximate unmodeled nonlinear dynamics online,
while an extended state observer (ESO) estimates the remaining
lumped disturbances for feedforward compensation. To evaluate the
contribution of each component, the proposed controller is tested
through backstepping-family ablation studies under nominal and
disturbed conditions. Simulation results show that the combined RBF-
ESO compensation improves both tracking accuracy and disturbance
rejection. The ablation results demonstrate that integrating neural
approximation and observer-based disturbance estimation within a
backstepping structure provides a balanced and robust control strategy
for multi-UAV cooperative payload transportation.

Keywords
multi-UAV cooperative transportation; payload transportation; back-
stepping control; RBF neural network; extended state observer

Introduction
UAV technology has developed rapidly in recent years and is now
widely used in aerial imaging, agriculture, industry, and material delivery.
Recently, increasing attention has been directed toward multi-UAV
systems. Compared with single-UAV platforms, multi-UAV cooperative
systems provide higher payload capacity, greater flexibility, and improved
fault tolerance. Among these applications, multi-UAV cooperative
payload transportation has attracted significant interest due to its potential
for transporting heavy and large-sized materials Fink et al. (2011); Lee
et al. (2013c); Estevez et al. (2024).
   However, cooperative payload transportation remains a challenging
problem in aerial robotics. The presence of flexible cables, coupled motion
constraints, and strong interactions among multiple UAVs and the payload
leads to a high-dimensional, nonlinear, and tightly coupled dynamical
system. In practice, these couplings introduce significant difficulties in
both controller design and stability analysis, especially when precise
trajectory tracking and attitude regulation are required simultaneously
Goodarzi et al. (2015); Sreenath et al. (2013); Lee (2018). Although a
complete geometric dynamic model has been established in prior work
Lee et al. (2013a); Lee (2014), achieving robust and high-accuracy control
under external disturbances, parameter uncertainties, and unmodeled
dynamics is still nontrivial. Therefore, developing control strategies that
combine accurate modeling with strong disturbance-rejection capability
remains an important research direction for cooperative multi-UAV
payload transportation.
   To address these challenges, a variety of control strategies have been
developed in the literature. Nonlinear methods, such as backstepping
and sliding-mode control, have been widely adopted for UAV systems
because of their ability to handle strong nonlinearities Bouabdallah and
Siegwart (2005); Madani and Benallegue (2008); Thanh et al. (2022).
Nevertheless, these approaches often depend on relatively accurate system
models, and their performance can degrade when significant parameter

have been introduced to approximate unknown dynamics online Sanner

These developments motivate the use of learning-based compensation

  In this paper, we develop an RBF-ESO backstepping controller for
multi-UAV cooperative payload transportation. Backstepping is adopted
as the nominal nonlinear tracking controller because it provides a recursive
error-stabilization structure for the payload translation and attitude
channels. An RBF neural network is then introduced to approximate
unmodeled nonlinear dynamics online Broomhead and Lowe (1988);
Park and Sandberg (1991); Shen and Xu (2021), while an ESO is used
to estimate the residual lumped disturbance that remains after neural
approximation Han (2009); Madoński and Herman (2015); Gu et al.
(2022). The resulting compensation terms are incorporated directly into
the payload force and moment commands.
  The main contribution of this paper is a simulation-oriented ablation
framework for evaluating the proposed composite controller. The
framework compares plain backstepping, RBF-augmented backstepping,
ESO-augmented backstepping, and the combined RBF-ESO backstepping
controller under both no-disturbance and disturbance-injection cases.
This organization makes it possible to identify the individual roles of
RBF approximation and ESO disturbance estimation within the same
cooperative transportation task.

System Modeling
The dynamic model used in this paper is based on the geometric
formulation proposed by Lee et al. for multiple quadrotor UAVs
transporting a cable-suspended rigid body Lee et al. (2013a,c). In that
framework, a cooperative aerial transportation system consists of N
quadrotors carrying a rigid payload through massless, taut, inextensible

Coordinate Frames and State Representation
We define three primary coordinate frames:
Inertial Frame (I ) A fixed Earth frame defined by basis vectors
{e1 , e2 , e3 }, where e3 = [0, 0, 1]T denotes the positive vertical axis; gravity
is represented as +ge3 under the NED convention.
Payload Body Frame (L) Attached to the payload center of mass (CoM),
with orientation RL ∈ SO(3) relative to I.
Quadrotor Body Frame (Bi ) Attached to the CoM of the i-th quadrotor
(i = 1 . . . N ), with orientation Ri ∈ SO(3) relative to I.
   For both the payload and each quadrotor, the dynamic variables are
position p, linear velocity v, attitude matrix R, and angular velocity Ω.
Here, p and v are expressed in the inertial frame, R ∈ SO(3) denotes
attitude, and Ω is expressed in the corresponding body frame. Subscripts
denote object indices: L for the payload, i for the i-th quadrotor (or cable),
and ext for external disturbances.

Hierarchical Control Design
To address uncertainties in payload dynamics and external disturbances,
we propose an adaptive robust control scheme that integrates backstepping
control, radial basis function (RBF) neural-network approximation, and
extended state observer (ESO) disturbance estimation while following the

geometric payload-transport control framework in Lee et al. (2013a); Lee
(2018). The backstepping component generates the nominal payload force
and moment commands, the RBF network compensates for unmodeled
nonlinear dynamics Sanner and Slotine (1992); Park and Sandberg (1991),
and the ESO estimates residual disturbances that are not fully captured by
the neural approximation Han (2009); Madoński and Herman (2015).

shown in Fig. 2. The payload tracking errors are first processed by the

online compensation for model uncertainty and residual disturbances. The
resulting payload-level force and moment commands are then distributed
through the cable-force allocation layer and converted into executable
quadrotor thrust and attitude commands.

estimation idea of ADRC/ESO-based control Han (2009); Gao (2003);
Madoński and Herman (2015); Gu et al. (2022).

acceleration form as

Stability Analysis
To verify the stability of the proposed composite controller under the
ideal payload-layer model, this subsection analyzes the ESO observation
error system and the closed-loop payload tracking error system based on
Lyapunov theory. The following assumptions are introduced.
  Assumption 1. The desired payload position, desired payload attitude,
and their first- and second-order derivatives are continuous and bounded.
  Assumption 2. For both the translational and rotational channels, the
unknown nonlinear terms can be approximated by optimal constant RBF
weights with bounded approximation errors.
  Assumption 3. The hidden-layer outputs of the RBF neural networks
are bounded.
  Assumption 4. The augmented disturbance in the translational channel
and the lumped disturbance in the rotational channel are continuously
differentiable, and their time derivatives are bounded.
  Assumption 5. The payload attitude trajectory remains in a compact
subset where the local attitude-coordinate representation used for the
rotational backstepping design is nonsingular.
  To eliminate the effect of the known gravity term on the translational
ESO error dynamics, the translational-channel augmented disturbance is
redefined accordingly. Then, the translational ESO can be written in a
standard augmented form.

Simulation Setup
A high-fidelity numerical simulation environment was developed in
MATLAB to assess the tracking performance and disturbance rejection
capability of the proposed cooperative load-lifting system. The simulated
platform consists of three identical quadrotors transporting a rigid
rectangular payload through three taut flexible cables with fixed nominal
length. The overall simulation is implemented as a hierarchical closed-
loop system comprising payload-level control, force allocation, cable-
direction regulation, and low-level quadrotor attitude control.

Physical Parameters and Numerical Integration
The coupled payload–quadrotor dynamics are integrated using a fixed-
step fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) scheme. A sampling interval of
∆t = 0.001 s is adopted to capture the fast attitude and cable-direction
dynamics, and each simulation runs for a total duration of Tf = 20 s.
  The principal numerical, physical, and geometric parameters used in
the simulations are listed in the Appendix. Unless otherwise stated, the
three quadrotors are identical, each with mass mp = 2.0 kg and inertia
matrix Jp = diag(0.082, 0.0845, 0.1377) kg · m2 . The payload mass is
mL = 2.0 kg, its geometric dimensions are 1.0 × 0.8 × 0.2 m, and its
inertia matrix is JL = diag(0.1133, 0.1733, 0.2733) kg · m2 .

Control Architecture and Disturbance Injection
To avoid repeating the previously introduced control pipeline, this
subsection focuses only on the disturbance setting used for robustness
evaluation. When disturbance injection is enabled, the translational and
rotational disturbance terms are modeled as

                  dp (t) = 3 sin t + 0.2 sin 2t + 0.3 sin 3t + 0.4 sin 4t,     (96)
                 dR (t) = 1 sin t + 0.2 sin 2t + 0.3 sin 3t + 0.4 sin 4t.      (97)
In the implemented model, the same scalar disturbance dp (t) is injected
into each translational channel of the payload dynamics, while dR (t) is
injected into each rotational channel.

Evaluation Protocol
The simulation study is organized as a backstepping-family ablation so
that the effect of each compensation module can be evaluated under
the same nominal controller structure. Four payload-level controllers are
evaluated under both no-disturbance and disturbance-injection conditions:
     • Backstepping, the nominal recursive nonlinear tracking controller;
     • RBF+Backstepping, where the RBF neural network is added for
       online approximation of unmodeled nonlinear dynamics;
     • ESO+Backstepping, where the ESO is added for online estimation
       of lumped residual disturbances;
     • RBF+ESO+Backstepping, the proposed composite controller
       combining neural approximation and observer-based disturbance
       compensation.
  All controllers are evaluated under the same physical parameters, initial
conditions, reference trajectory, and disturbance model. The reported

over the final 80% of the stored simulation data. The controller and

  From Fig. 4, it can be observed that all backstepping-family controllers

Backstepping controller exhibits relatively larger attitude deviations
during the initial stage, especially in the roll and pitch channels, while
the controllers with RBF or ESO compensation reduce the transient

controller shows the fastest attenuation of the initial position and attitude
errors and maintains nearly zero steady-state errors after the transient
process. This indicates that even in the nominal case, the additional
compensation modules help suppress the effects of nonlinear coupling and
allocation-induced modeling errors.

Conclusion
This paper presented an RBF-ESO backstepping control framework for
multi-UAV cooperative payload transportation. The proposed controller
uses backstepping to construct the nominal payload force and moment
commands, an RBF neural network to approximate unmodeled nonlinear
dynamics, and an ESO to estimate residual lumped disturbances online.
   The simulation framework was rebuilt around a backstepping-
family ablation study. Under no-disturbance and disturbance-injection
conditions, the paper compares plain Backstepping, RBF+Backstepping,
ESO+Backstepping, and RBF+ESO+Backstepping, which clarifies the
individual contributions of the RBF and ESO modules. The results
indicate that combining neural approximation with observer-based
disturbance compensation improves tracking accuracy and disturbance
rejection for the coupled aerial payload transportation system.
   Overall, the proposed composite control structure provides a
systematic way to integrate model-based backstepping, neural-network
approximation, and observer-based disturbance estimation within a
cooperative payload-transport framework. The ablation results show that
the RBF and ESO components complement the nominal backstepping
controller, yielding improved robustness without sacrificing attitude-
tracking performance. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of
the proposed method for accurate and robust multi-UAV payload
transportation under nonlinear coupling and external disturbances.
